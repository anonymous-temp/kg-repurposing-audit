## S8. Audit of the evaluation used in software release v0.2.0

The present protocol replaces the Hetionet evaluation of our earlier software release (v0.2.0). This section documents the problems that motivated the change, so that results obtained with the earlier code can be interpreted. All checks used the public Hetionet v1.0 files and the v0.2.0 code; the reconstruction scripts are in the repository (legacy/v0.2 and scripts/audit_v02).

**Partitions.** The random-edge and compound-disjoint partitions of v0.2.0 were reconstructed exactly from its random-number call order (604/151 and 593/162 fitting/test positives). Their test sets are the seed-42 test sets of the present study.

**Uniform candidate pools.** v0.2.0 drew uniform negatives from the product of the held-out compounds and all 137 disease nodes. Sixty of the 137 diseases have no treatment edge anywhere in Hetionet; 43.7% of uniformly sampled negatives fell on these diseases and could be recognised from disease degree alone. The present grids include only the 77 diseases with at least one recorded treatment.

**Disease-frequency candidate pools.** The second v0.2.0 sampler drew diseases from the multiset of test-positive diseases and drew compounds uniformly from the held-out compounds. It rejected known positives and repeated pairs and made at most 50 attempts per requested negative. Because only 77–116 compounds were held out, frequent diseases could not supply 20 or 30 distinct negatives per positive. In the compound-disjoint task, the pool contained 4,381 pairs. This is exactly the number of unlabelled pairs in the grid of 77 held-out compounds and 59 test-positive diseases (77 × 59 − 162), so the sampler exhausted the grid and the intended frequency weighting was lost. Under this sampler, the disease-only reference kept an AUROC of 0.62–0.67, against 0.51 under the joint degree-bin matching used for PrimeKG in v0.2.0. The total-variation distance between the disease distributions of positives and sampled negatives was 0.25 (random-edge) and 0.31 (compound-disjoint).

**Tail corruption.** The v0.2.0 embedding code drew corrupted tails uniformly over all 47,031 entities, of which 137 (0.29%) are diseases. During training, about 0.3% of the corruptions of treatment edges were therefore diseases. We re-implemented the v0.2.0 training loop on the seed-42 random-edge partition and evaluated it on reconstructed v0.2.0 candidate pools. Changing only the corruption scheme to type-constrained sampling raised DistMult AP under uniform candidates from 0.151 to 0.248 (seed 1) and from 0.141 to 0.207 (seed 2). Under disease-frequency candidates, AP rose from 0.095 to 0.128 and from 0.079 to 0.112. The corresponding degree-reference AP was 0.211 and 0.100 (mean of two seeds). Section S7 repeats the comparison on the full evaluation grid.

**Label-only baseline.** The v0.2.0 semi-synthetic write-back experiment fitted a 16-dimensional logistic matrix factorisation without graph information on the same partition and candidate pools. Without write-back, its AP was 0.403 (uniform) and 0.233 (disease frequency), compared with at most 0.161 and 0.106 for the four embedding models reported for the same cells. A rerun of that code on the reconstructed partition gave 0.365 and 0.211.

**Consequences.** The v0.2.0 comparisons of embedding models with the degree reference on Hetionet reflected the corruption scheme and the candidate construction rather than properties of the model families. The present protocol therefore uses type-constrained corruption, graph embeddings trained without treatment edges, and label-only and graph-informed scorers side by side. It scores full candidate grids restricted to labelled diseases, uses five partitions per task and summarises uncertainty with a disease-cluster bootstrap of per-disease AP, which is insensitive to changes in positive fraction across draws.
